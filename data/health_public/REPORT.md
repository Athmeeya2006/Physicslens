## 1. Bearings: which defect rate stands out (UORED-VAFCLS, 60 recordings)

Rule (fixed before scoring): Envelope spectrum of the band with the highest kurtosis. For each defect rate (BPFO, BPFI, BSF, FTF) take harmonics 1 to 3; a harmonic stands out when the peak within +-1% (at least +-0.5 Hz) of the expected line, ignoring bins within 0.3 Hz of a shaft harmonic, is at least 6 dB above the median of the spectrum within 10 Hz. A defect is present when at least 2 of its 3 harmonics stand out; the diagnosis is the present defect with the highest mean peak level over its harmonics, or 'none' when no defect is present.

Shaft rate from the 1x vibration line agrees with the Hall-sensor speed within 2.98% on every recording.

### Post hoc second rule (written after the first failed; not used for the status)

Rule: Shaft rate: the 1x vibration line within the motor's slip range (synchronous speed down to 10% slip), interpolated. Envelope spectra of the test recording and of the same bearing's healthy recording, both in the test recording's highest-kurtosis band. A defect series grew when at least 2 of its 3 harmonics both stand out in the test recording (at least 6 dB over local background) and rose at least 6 dB over the healthy recording; the diagnosis is the grown series with the largest mean rise, or 'none'.

Why it was tried: the Hall-sensor speed stored once per file differs from the measured 1x vibration line by 1.9% (median) and up to 23%, and a healthy recording can already show a defect series (the motor's own internal bearings have the same geometry). Thresholds were not changed. Measured shaft speeds span 1632.5 to 1803.5 rpm; the lowest values (some cage-bearing recordings, near the bottom of the slip range) suggest the strongest line there is not the 1x line, which is not resolved.

- faulty: type correct 3/20 (15%, 95% 5% to 36%); any fault detected 8/20 (40%, 95% 22% to 61%); per type outer 2/5, inner 1/5, ball 0/5, cage 0/5.
- developing: type correct 3/20 (15%, 95% 5% to 36%); any fault detected 10/20 (50%, 95% 30% to 70%); per type outer 2/5, inner 0/5, ball 0/5, cage 1/5.
- healthy (second half against first half): false alarms 0/20 (0%, 95% 0% to 16%).

It removes the false alarms but does not find the fault type either. On this dataset, envelope analysis at the kinematic defect rates does not identify the fault type; one possible reason, not tested here, is that the run-to-failure damage of degreased bearings is distributed wear rather than a single localized pit, and distributed wear has no single impact rate.
